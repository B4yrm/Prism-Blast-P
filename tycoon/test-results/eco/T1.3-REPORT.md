design: `docs/ECONOMY-ARCHITECTURE.md` §3. Goal: follow the species of the fish through the existing flow without any effect on the economy.

Eco is **off by default**; it is switched on in a development build with `?eco=1` or `__eco.setOn(true)`.

## 2. How the species are followed, and what is exact

The simulation has no species. It counts whole fish: `S.stock.*` per place, a boat's `load`, a hauler's `carry`, and the conveyor queue.
The ledger keeps, for each of those fish, 5 quantities (one per species) and moves them when the simulation moves the fish.

**Why the bus events are not enough:** `unload` does not say how many fish; there is no event for a pick or for a conveyor entry; `drop` reports
the carry before the stock was capped. **What is enough:** every write to `S.stock` lives in exactly one of four `Sim` methods, and each method makes
one kind of write per call. So the stock before and after a call says exactly what the call did. The adapter observes those four calls, read-only
(the same mechanism as the approved T1.1 wrapper of `Sim.prototype.update`).
This is proven, not assumed, by `tests/eco/stock-writers.cjs` (§4, test 1).

| Move | How it is read | Species | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Catch** (`fish` → `back`) | boat state change, `load` = the catch | decided by eco from the `catch` stream and the catch weights; not read from the simulation | **exact amount; species are an eco model** |
| **Boat → quay** (unload) | `load` decrease = the amount, in the boat's dock state | the boat's cargo composition | **exact amount.** A partial unload takes the average of what is left aboard; the last part takes the rest exactly. |
| **Quay / warehouse / factory output → hauler** (pick) | stock decrease of the source = the amount | average composition of the pool | **average mix** (the simulation has no individual fish in a pool) |
| **Hauler → place** (drop) | stock change of the destination | the batch picked up | **exact:** the batch keeps its composition. A delivery cut short by a full place: the amount that arrived is exact, the rest is counted as lost with its species. |
| **Warehouse → conveyor** (entry) | warehouse decrease | average composition of the warehouse | **average mix** |
| **Conveyor → factory output** (exit) | factory output increase | the composition the unit had when it entered, FIFO | **exact** |
| **Shelf → customer** (sale) | shelf decrease | average composition of the shelf | **average mix**; totals per species are in the ledger |
| Fish in transit when the game is closed | lost by the simulation | lost (same as the simulation) | not saved, not invented |
| Tracking started in the middle of a session | boats, haulers, conveyor units already loaded | assumed (catch weights / composition of the source) | **assumed, counted** in `ledger.assumed` (0 in normal runs) |

Not part of T1.3: product types and recipes (T3.1), quality and spoilage (T2.5), prices (T1.4), offline earnings (they move no fish; T2.6).
`omix` records which **species** processed units were made from; it says nothing about product types.

**Rounding:** quantities are fractional inside the ledger (a pool of 40 fish that is 55 % sardine holds 22 sardines; taking 5 fish out
removes 2.75). Whole numbers are only shown, with `roundCounts`, which sums to the stock exactly, so rounding never creates or removes a fish.

**Nothing is made up:** a move that cannot be matched with a record (an unload without cargo, a drop without a carried batch, a conveyor exit without a unit,
a stock that no longer matches) is a *violation*: it is counted, the first 20 are kept, one `ECOERR species …` line is logged. Nothing is repaired silently.

**What is saved:** `S.eco.mix` (quay, wh, mRaw) and `S.eco.omix` (facOut, mProd) as shares, and the `catch` stream. An empty place gets the reference mix
and quality 1, as the T1.2 reconcile rules require, so a saved game never needs a repair for it. The cumulative ledger and the boats' / haulers' batches are in memory only.

## 5. Species inventory: invariants and what they proved

Every step of every run in test 5 is audited with an independent checker (not the tracker's own):
(a) the species of every place sum to the simulation's stock; (b) a boat's cargo equals its load while it carries a catch; (c) a hauler's batch equals its carry;
(d) the conveyor's units equal the simulation's queue; (e) no negative quantity; (f) per species, *initial + caught + assumed = in pools + aboard + carried + on the conveyor + sold + lost*
(so no fish is created, lost, or counted twice).

| Scenario | Result |
|---|---|
| A. start / mid / high / max, 600 s each, 3 modes each (no adapter, eco off, eco on) | Audits clean at every step. The simulation (state every 0.5 s, the final save, **and the number of draws from the global random generator**) is identical in all three modes. Ledger **caught** total = the simulation's own `stats.caught` increase, **sold** total = its `stats.sold` increase, exactly. Lost = 0. Species not yet unlocked never appear (start: only 3 species; tier 3: all 5 caught and sold). Whole-number counts sum to every stock. |
| B. tuna only | All other species are exactly 0 in every place, boat, hauler batch and conveyor unit at every step; everything sold equals the simulation's sold counter. |
| C. two disjoint boat groups (boats 0, 2: sardine only; boats 1, 3: tuna only) | A boat's cargo never holds the other group's species; caught per species equals the loads the simulation's boats brought in (1,180 / 1,190). |
| D. no haulers | Partial unloads (344 steps) and boats held up by a full quay with their catch aboard (39,442 boat-steps, up to 28 fish aboard) audit clean; haulers added later start moving the stock. |
| E. warehouse capacity lowered while haulers carry | Deliveries cut short (2), 51 fish lost, **counted with their species**; audits clean. |
| F. tracking started in the middle of a session | Assumed = exactly what was loaded at that moment (41 aboard + 4 carried + 3 on the conveyor); audited clean afterwards. |
| G. on → off → on | While off, S.eco is byte-identical; tracking restarts from the saved composition. |
| H. save → fresh modules → load → continue | S.eco loads with 0 repairs and is not changed by loading; composition equal after the reload; audits clean for another 300 s. |
| I. mutation: remove any one observer | The audits report errors every time (4/4), so the audits do detect a missing observation. |
| In the page (test 6), mid and max, 300 s @30 FPS, audit every 2 s | Identical to base; no violation; nothing assumed; all 7 kinds of move seen (e.g. max: 118 catches, 1,168 unloads, 622 picks, 619 drops, 976 entries, 976 exits, 132 sales). |

## 7. Boat sync: base and eco side by side

`boat-sync.cjs` runs 17 scenarios and checks, among others, that the simulation is unchanged, that boats never overlap or touch land, and that each drawn boat is back in its slip when the simulation starts unloading.
Its start-up frames run in real time (its own header says so), so the same build gives different numbers on different runs.
In every run below, base and eco ran **at the same time** on the same machine.

| round | build | scenarios passed | hard checks | timing failures (check, how late) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | base | 17/17 | all pass | - |
| 1 | eco, tracking on | 14/17 | all pass | start-fleet4: `slipTripsInBeforeUnloading` (1.77 s); mid-fleet4: `slipTripsInBeforeUnloading` (3.43 s); mid+-fleet4: `curveTripsInTime` |
| 2 | base | 16/17 | all pass | mid-fleet4: `slipTripsInBeforeUnloading` (2.4 s) |
| 2 | eco, tracking on | 16/17 | all pass | start-fleet4: `slipTripsInBeforeUnloading` (1.73 s) |
| 3 | base | 17/17 | all pass | - |
| 3 | eco, tracking on | 16/17 | all pass | mid-fleet4: `slipTripsInBeforeUnloading` (2.03 s) |
| 4 | base | 15/17 | all pass | mid-fleet4: `slipTripsInBeforeUnloading` (2.63 s); mid+-fleet4: `curveTripsInTime` |
| 4 | eco, tracking on | 16/17 | all pass | mid-fleet4: `slipTripsInBeforeUnloading` (1.87 s) |
| **total** | base | **65/68** | | 3 timing failures |
| **total** | eco, tracking on | **62/68** | | 6 timing failures (3 of them in round 1; rounds 2 to 4: 3 vs 3 for base) |

"Hard checks" = `simulationUnchanged`, `noPageError`, `noOverlap`, `noContact`, `hullsAQuarterBeamApart`. Earlier, sequential T1.2 runs of the same test: eco off 16/17 twice, the unchanged base build 15/17 twice.

**Judgement:** `simulationUnchanged`, `noPageError`, `noOverlap`, `noContact` and `hullsAQuarterBeamApart` passed in **every scenario of every run, base and eco**.
The only failures are the timing checks `slipTripsInBeforeUnloading` and `curveTripsInTime`, and they occur on both sides and move from scenario to scenario.
The eco side had more of them in total (6 against 3 in 68 scenario-runs), but the excess is all in round 1; in rounds 2 to 4 both sides have 3.
The deterministic test 15 (same frozen start, 1,800 samples per scenario) found **no difference at all** in how the boats are drawn in base, eco off and eco on, so eco does not change how a drawn boat moves:
the differences of boat-sync come from its real-time start-up. **What this cannot prove:** a very small real effect on those real-time start-up frames (tracking adds about 1 µs per step) cannot be excluded
with 4 rounds of a noisy test. The check was not loosened and the test was not changed.

## 9. Problems found

1. **My in-page audit had an operator-precedence bug** (`!Math.abs(x) < 1e-9`), which flagged every shares-sum that was not exactly 1 and stopped the stepping early. That made the first run of test 6 fail (identity "differences" at 38 s and 268 s were just the stopped run). Fixed in the test; no game code was involved. The stepping now never stops early.
2. Three other test-script mistakes were found and fixed on the way (a missing `stat` export, in-flight fish removed from the stock after tracking had started, a hand-made boat without `load`), plus an arbitrary threshold in test B, replaced by an exact check against the simulation's own counter.
3. **A delivery can be cut short only if a place's capacity is lowered** (the normal flow reserves space, so it never happens in the 6 normal scenarios; the one cut-short drop in test 1 comes from the lowered capacity). The ledger handles both cases (amount that arrived, lost remainder, and the cut of the stock itself) and tests them.
4. **Boat-sync is noisy** (§7).
5. **The existing `save-compare.cjs` reports a failure by design** now that the save has a new key; use `save-compare-eco.cjs` for the real check.

## 10. Open points and risks

- **Catch weights for harbor tiers 2 and 3 are placeholders** (`defs-eco.js`, balance assumption). Tier 1 is the GDD reference mix. They only change which species the fish are; no price, income or timing reads them. T1.4 / T1.5 will set the real values (seasons, abundance, unlock).
- **Average-mix moves are by definition** (§2). If a later system needs the exact species of individual fish in a pool (for example "sell only sea bass"), the simulation would need identities for fish. That is a decision for later; the game's selective-sale policy is already out of V1 (Q12).
- **The cumulative ledger and in-flight batches are not saved**; after a reload tracking starts from the saved composition (and the simulation also loses what was in flight). The ledger totals (`caught`, `sold`) are therefore per session.
- **Eco is off by default and only in dev builds.** Nothing in a production build, the APK or CI changed. Switching it on for players is a later decision (roadmap Q11).
- **Per-method observation goes one step beyond the events** (§2). It uses the same mechanism as the approved wrapper of `Sim.update`. If you prefer not to wrap those four methods, the alternative is a small `bus.emit` in `src/sim/sim.js` for each of the four moves (4 lines, **protected file**, needs your approval and a new regression proof).
- The adapter now depends on four more simulation method names and a few fields (`b.state`, `b.load`, `w.state`, `w.job.src/dst`, `w.carry`, `convey`). `adapter-contract.cjs` fails if they change.
- No Android measurement of tracking (Q10 still open).

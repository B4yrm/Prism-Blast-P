# T1.4: species base prices, balance-neutral

Date: 2026-11-15. Branch `claude/clever-maxwell-tolun5`, starting from `e7a7424` (T1.3). Plan: `docs/ROADMAP-28-DAYS.md` T1.4;
design: GDD §3.1, `docs/ECONOMY-ARCHITECTURE.md` §3.3 and §8.1.

**Result:** with eco on, the fresh-fish price follows the species on the shelf; at the start mix the factor is exactly 1.000 and the
600 s revenue differs by +0.6 % on average (−2.4 % … +2.9 % over 8 seeds), within the ±10 % acceptance. With eco off nothing changes.
No protected file was touched; save and offline needed no change (the stop condition was not triggered).
No other system was activated (no market, weather or season). Eco stays **off by default** (dev builds: `?eco=1`).

## 2. Price factor of each species and the normalisation

```
value(mix)  = Σ share_i × base_i                                  (shares of the 5 species; quantities are divided by their sum)
factor(mix) = clamp( value(mix) / value(start mix), 0.4, 2.5 )
value(start mix) = 0.55 × 0.6 + 0.35 × 0.9 + 0.10 × 1.4 = 0.785   (the reference start mix of GDD §3.1 = catch weights of harbor tier 1)
fresh-fish price with eco on = original rawPrice × factor          (original = 4 × cold storage × market price × brand × boost)
```

**Why normalise:** the original game sells "fresh fish" at one price. Dividing by the value of the start mix makes that mix worth exactly what fresh
fish is worth today (factor 1.000, so a new game earns the same), while the species keep their real differences against each other.
The species differences are not removed: a sardine is still worth 0.6/3.5 of a tuna.

| species | base | factor alone, before the limit | factor alone, applied |
|---|---|---|---|
| sardine | 0.6 | ×0.7643 | ×0.7643 |
| mackerel | 0.9 | ×1.1465 | ×1.1465 |
| sea bass | 1.4 | ×1.7834 | ×1.7834 |
| salmon | 2.0 | ×2.5478 | **×2.5** (limited) |
| tuna | 3.5 | ×4.4586 | **×2.5** (limited) |
| start mix (tier 1 catch) | | ×1.0000 | **×1.0000 exactly** |
| tier 2 catch mix (placeholder weights) | | ×1.1439 | ×1.1439 |
| tier 3 catch mix (placeholder weights) | | ×1.3898 | ×1.3898 |

The limit [0.4, 2.5] is the one of architecture §3.3. It binds only for a mix worth more than 2.5 × the start mix (salmon or tuna nearly alone).

**Which mix is used where.** During a sale (inside `Sim.updCustomers`) the factor is that of the species actually on the fresh-fish shelf: a sale takes the shelf's
average mix (T1.3). Everywhere else (`steadyRate`, and through it the shop's time warp, purse and chest values, and the UI's price display) it is the factor of
the expected mix, the catch weights of the harbor tier, i.e. the long-run mix of the fish that come in.

## 3. Offline earnings and double counting

- The offline earnings are computed by `main.js` when the page loads: `steadyRate() × seconds × 0.5`. Eco switches on later, at `worldReady`.
  So **the offline amount uses the original price, with or without eco.** Eco adds no offline amount of its own, so nothing can be counted twice.
  Proven in the page with a save where the factor is not 1 (harbor tier 3, all fish sold fresh, one hour away): the offline offer is **+$5.40K in all four modes**
  (base, eco off, tracking only, tracking + prices), while prices are at work in that save (factor ×1.3898 after 60 s, register 28 → 40).
- During play with eco on, `steadyRate()` is multiplied by the factor **once** (one multiplication inside `rawPrice`). Example, harbor tier 3 without a factory:
  steadyRate 3.000 → 4.169 $/s (×1.3898), the offline formula would give 5,400 → 7,504 per hour away, not ×1.3898² (checked).
  With a factory, `steadyRate` uses the product price and does not change at all (11,880 = 11,880).
- **Consequence to note:** with eco on at harbor tiers 2 and 3, the shop's time warp / purse / chest (computed during play) use the higher expected price, while
  the offline amount at load does not. That is consistent with T1.4's rule "offline unchanged", and should be decided when eco's own offline rules come (T2.6).

## 4. Balance simulation (`tools/balance-sim.mjs`, full results in `t1.4/balance-sim.md` / `.json`)

The real simulation runs from the same start state with the same seeds, once with the original economy and once with eco prices. The till is collected every
200 steps, so both runs sell exactly the same units at the same moments (checked, "flows identical"): a paired comparison with no simulation noise.
Ratio = money earned with eco prices ÷ original.

| scenario | seconds × seeds | mean | min … max | fresh fish in revenue | realised fresh-fish factor (before rounding) | flows identical |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **start, reference mix (tier 1, no factory)** | **600 × 8** | **×1.0056 (+0.6 %)** | ×0.9761 … ×1.0292 | 100 % | ×1.0014 | yes |
| start, reference mix, long run | 1800 × 4 | ×1.0068 (+0.7 %) | ×1.0008 … ×1.0139 | 100 % | ×1.0039 | yes |
| start, sardine only | 600 × 4 | ×0.7500 (−25 %) | ×0.7500 | 100 % | ×0.7643 | yes |
| start, tuna only | 600 × 4 | ×2.5000 (+150 %) | ×2.5000 | 100 % | ×2.5000 | yes |
| mid: tier 3 mix, factory, long run | 1800 × 4 | ×1.0297 (+3.0 %) | ×1.0253 … ×1.0332 | 7 % | ×1.3999 | yes |
| high: tier 3 mix, factory tier 3 | 600 × 4 | ×1.0000 | ×1.0000 | 0 % | no fresh fish sold | yes |
| tier 3 mix, no factory (everything sold fresh) | 600 × 4 | ×1.3940 (+39.4 %) | ×1.3599 … ×1.4192 | 100 % | ×1.3892 | yes |

**Acceptance: PASS.** Start mix within ±10 % for every seed (worst −2.4 %) and the mean; eco off identical to the original (state every 0.5 s, final save,
global-random draws) for all 8 seeds; flows identical in every run; no bad price.

Why the start-mix ratio is not exactly 1: the catches are random draws from the weights, so the shelf holds what happened to arrive (realised factor ×1.0014),
and the game rounds every sale to whole money.

## 8. Problems found

1. **Rounding of the game moves small prices.** `Sim.serve` rounds every sale to whole money. At the start a fresh fish costs 4, so one sardine sells for
   round(4 × 0.764) = 3: an effective ×0.75 instead of ×0.764. In mixed shelves this rounding averages out (start mix +0.6 %), but for one cheap species alone it is visible.
   It shrinks as prices grow with upgrades. This is the game's own rounding (protected code), not changed.
2. **The limit flattens salmon and tuna.** Alone, salmon (×2.548) and tuna (×4.459) both become ×2.5. In realistic mixes the limit does not bind
   (tier 3 mix ×1.39). Whether pure tuna should be worth more than 2.5× is a balance decision (see §9).
3. Two mistakes in my own test scripts were found and fixed: a wrong lower bound for sardine (the rounding above), and a harness that made two worlds before playing
   them, which left the second run on an unseeded random generator (results varied 0.73–0.76 between runs). After the fix, three runs give identical results.
4. The existing workers regression and the drawn-boats test do not empty the till, so with prices on they diverge through the "till full" rule (§6). That is the game's rule,
   not an error; the checks with a collected till are clean.

## 9. Open risks and decisions

- **Harbor tiers 2 and 3 earn more with eco on:** the expected factor is ×1.144 and ×1.390 (placeholder catch weights from T1.3). A player who sells all fish fresh
  at tier 3 would earn about +39 %. With a factory the effect is small (fresh fish ≈ 7 % of revenue). The weights must be set in T1.5 (seasons, unlocks), or the tiers
  normalised separately, before eco can be on for players.
- **Limit 2.5:** keeps the economy safe, but pure tuna is worth the same as pure salmon (§8.2).
- **Offline vs during play** (§3): offline unchanged by design; the shop values during play use the factor. Needs a rule in T2.6.
- **Products keep their price** until recipes (T3.1); species of origin are tracked (`omix`) but not priced.
- **Rounding at low prices** (§8.1) is the game's own behaviour.
- **Boat sync stays noisy** (§7). The deterministic drawn-boats test is the reliable check.
- No Android measurement (Q10 still open).
